Dr. Shailesh Kumar, Chief Data Scientist at Jio and Dean of Jio Institute, gave this keynote at Wayfair AI Nexus in Bengaluru on 9 Oct 2026. The talk was titled “Intelligent operating system as a service”. This is the talk in his words, lightly edited from a recording, followed by the Q&A. Written up by Varun Tulsian, pending his permission and review.

Automation, intelligence, integration#

The first wave was automation. The second wave is what we are in today, which is intelligence, right? And the third wave is integration. So today I will talk about the third wave, and show you the journey from automation to intelligence to integration.

When you think of the word integration, what comes to my mind is the word operating system, right? Operating system as a service. SaaS is going to die. OSaaS is going to come.

Automation, then intelligence, then integration. Integration means an operating system: OSaaS.

The grid, not the bulbs#

Think about electricity. Electricity has become a platform, and all our appliances run on top of it. But how did we get to electricity? Did we get to electricity by building bulbs and solar panels and transformers and switches and wires? We didn’t get to electricity by solving all the use cases of it.

The secret sauce is not that we solved all the use cases. The secret is that we built a grid, which connects the production, the distribution and the consumption of electricity. The grid is important, right? Not the use cases. So this top-down thinking is the theme of the day today, and I will talk about the two mindsets.

We did not get electricity by building bulbs. We got it by building the grid that connects production, distribution and consumption.

Mother Teresa or Buddha#

So one key idea I talk about now is what is called meta-thinking, right? I also get involved with teaching, so let me talk about a great innovator. I will put Buddha and innovator in the same sentence, and I want to highlight three qualities.

So what happened? Buddha was going to become a king. He was going to run a kingdom. And suddenly he decided to go out and visit the city, and he saw a sick man, an old man and a dead man. And he realized that this is the right problem to solve. Not running a kingdom. The bigger problem, which is human suffering.

So that is what a great innovator does. The first quality is to find the right problem to solve, right? Is it search? Is it LLMs? Is it supply chain? What is the right problem to solve in your domain? That is the first one.

The second quality. Did he know how to solve the problem when he decided to solve it? No. Learning and education come after you decide what you want to solve. That’s what we do. We learn how to solve the problem.

And the third quality. Compare him with the other approach to human suffering, which is Mother Teresa. Her approach was one person at a time, one use case at a time. Buddha’s approach was to solve it for all mankind, forever. That is a platform. So there is a services approach and there is a platform approach, and these two great thinkers show us both.

If you look at Infosys, Wipro, TCS, they are Mother Teresa. They solve one use case for one bank, and another use case for another bank. So who is the Buddha company? Claude Code is a Buddha company. It said, I am going to solve all programming problems for everyone, forever. That is the contrast.

Mother Teresa companies solve one use case at a time. Buddha companies solve it for everyone, forever.

Reimagine#

The other mindset is what I call reimagination. We are surrounded by amazing technologies, and there are always two types of people in a room. Some of us will go deep into one technology. I am going to improve search forever. I am going to improve LLMs forever. I am going to improve 3D printers forever. You are the thinkers, and you want to go deep. But the second kind of innovators are the ones who created Wayfair, Ola, Uber, Swiggy, the platforms. They use all these technologies to do one thing, which is to reimagine the world, given where we are today.

Reimagine is a very different skill. Reimagine means: if I were born today, how would I do what we are doing today, differently? How would education look in today’s world? Just think about that question for a second. You will realize our current education system is archaic compared to what can be done.

Let me give you an example from your context, right? How do we buy a chair today? You go search, you go try things out, and all that. Let me reimagine how we will buy a chair in the future, and this could be a clue for how you think about research.

Imagine you go to ChatGPT, or some new software, and say: design a chair for me. My height, my weight, my back problems and all of that. It designs a perfect chair just for you. Then you say, okay, I want to try it. So you try it out in AR, and you like it. But that chair doesn’t exist in any supply chain. It is a unique chair design, just for you. What are you going to do? There is a print button. There is a 3D printer that can print it. Have I disrupted the whole thing? Anything that can be printed on a 3D printer should never be manufactured and put in a supply chain.

So search will be replaced by design, and supply chain will be replaced by 3D printing. We can reimagine 80% of the retail business like this. That is the new skill you want to think with.

Let me give you another example. Here is a sentence. When wireless is perfectly applied, the whole earth will be a single brain. We will communicate instantly. We will see each other face to face. The instruments will be very small. This is exactly how our world looks today, right? We are one whole brain, and all of this is true. Who said it? Go back and look. Tesla said it, in 1926. That is the power of reimagination. Somebody imagined in 1926 that the world is going to be like this. That is the kind of new skill we want to produce.

Why companies die and survive#

Transformation is so important. Why do companies die, and why do they survive? Let me tell you a story. Once upon a time, you went to a store and rented a bunch of movies to watch on the weekend. You did not return them on time, and you paid a fine. Remember that? Then a new company came in and asked: why should you go to a store? Why can’t the movies come to you? Netflix started sending the movies to your home. You can keep them as long as you want.

What he did was understand the customer pain point and change the business process. And in the process he created a digital-physical company. You go to a website, look for the movies you want, order them there, and they send them to you. The first digital-physical company.

While he did that, he did one more thing. He became a data company. He started collecting data on who is buying what movies, and he built the first recommendation engine. If you like this, you will also like that. You see, a little change in the business process led him to become a data company. That is a great example, and it is a case study in business schools.

Here is another case study. Do you know who invented the first digital camera? Kodak. And Kodak said no. Our current business is doing well. Why should we let this technology take over? Nobody will want digital photographs. This is how backward the thinking was. Had Kodak pioneered the digital camera, they could have owned Google Photos, YouTube, Pinterest, everything we do with images today. This is how important it is to think about digital transformation. If you are not going to disrupt your own business, somebody else will. I am sure somebody is thinking about 3D printers and printing chairs. If you are not thinking about it, they will take over.

From point solutions to a health operating system#

Let me take five companies in healthcare. One helped you diagnose your disease, given your symptoms. It closed down. Practo in India helps you find the right doctor. Another has labs that send people to your home to collect samples. Another delivers medicines. What is wrong with this? We are all solving the bulbs and the switches and the wires. Nobody is building the electricity. What we should be building is what I call the health operating system, right?

Today I am a patient. I don’t want to go to four apps, one to find what is wrong, one to book an appointment, one for the lab. How do I move from a point-solution mindset to a platform mindset?

And what is wrong with this? This is MyJio. This is Paytm. This is Bajaj Finance. What are they doing? They are saying, I want to show all the things I offer in a single UI. With no connection in the background. That is not what they were supposed to do. They were supposed to build a super-app. That is the shift in mindset we want to talk about.

The last four digital platforms#

So let me now talk about the last four digital platforms mankind will build. Once we build these four, we won’t need to have conferences like this anymore. Like electricity: nobody holds electricity conferences. Hopefully we will not need these either. So what are these four operating systems? And please challenge me over lunch if I missed something.

The first one is what I call the domain operating system. How do we run a retail business, a telecom business, a banking business, a healthcare business, right? We need an operating system mindset. Because what do Walmart and Flipkart and Amazon do? Essentially, as retailers, they do the same five or six workflows. So can somebody write a retail operating system once, and say: you can customize it according to you? We need domain operating systems. Right now there is big chaos, with every company trying to build its own version. Every bank has an IT team and an AI team. It’s like 200 years ago, when every company was producing its own electricity. Then somebody realized that electricity is a horizontal that everybody needs. An operating system for a domain is a horizontal. Every company in the domain needs the same operating system, customized.

The second is what I call the life operating system, the super-app. Your phone today has hundreds of apps, for food, travel and all of that. How do we do integration today? When you are hungry, you don’t think about the restaurant, you think about Zomato or Swiggy, right? This is how we have changed. So how do we integrate our life? A super-app.

The third is the work super-app. Think about what a lawyer does in a day, or a teacher, or a doctor. Their lives are very difficult, but nobody has built a product just for their role. What is the lawyer super-app going to look like? Salesforce is an example of a work super-app for salespeople. The Uber driver app is an example of a work super-app, right? Everything is in one place. Everybody else uses generic products, like Excel, Gmail, WhatsApp and calls, to do a very specific job.

And the last one I will talk about is the AI marketplace. AI is a horizontal, everybody needs it. Why should everybody build it? That is like saying everybody grow your own food, or everybody generate your own electricity. Society works horizontally.

This, I think, is a one-trillion-dollar idea. If somebody can build all these four, these will be the last four digital platforms.

Four operating systems: domain OS, life super-app, work super-app and AI marketplace. Build all four, and these are the last digital platforms.

The life super-app#

So the higher-order thinking we are learning is integration as a mindset, right? Life operating system. This is the rise of the super-app. Why do you think Musk bought Twitter? It was not because Twitter was his goal. He wanted to build X, the everything app, and Grok is part of it. So the life super-app will be the last B2C product. And look at what companies are trying to do. Alexa+, Tata Neu, they are all trying to become super-apps. Maybe they know it, maybe not, but they have not executed it yet. They all came from different places, and they are all trying to become a super-app.

So here is how a super-app is constructed. We are calling it Omni. Omni has an interface which is universal: all languages, all modalities. You can take a picture, you can speak, you can walk into a mall and it recognizes where you are. And it knows your full context. Let’s say you are going to Delhi, and you don’t have your medicine with you. It knows. That is the real context. Not a 10-million-token context. Real context, human context, what is going on in your life.

Think about it. Ola and Uber know you only from your rides. Zomato knows you only from your food. But because now you are doing everything via the super-app, it knows you holistically. And that is where the magic is going to come. Why would somebody go there? Exactly why people go to Amazon. I get everything in one place. I want to say: hey, I am hungry. Or I have fever. Or I want to buy a sofa. Or I want to study this concept. Or I want to make a call. You go to one place.

So you will have only one life super-app and one work super-app on your phone. Just two super-apps.

And here is what it looks like. Imagine you are sitting in office, and your super-app says: it’s time to go home, right? It pushes. That is a very important shift. Today, when Netflix or YouTube personalize, they push. When you search, you pull. Both are available. And now imagine you say: in 30 minutes. It invokes an agent. It looks at your data, it looks at all the partners, cabs that are too expensive, too far, too costly, tries this, tries that, and books it. You never have to open an app. This looks very simple, but it is very powerful as a construct.

When to use an agent#

Now let me talk a little bit about how you would build it. What do you build and how do you build are two different things. So let me talk about how.

Imagine I take one use case, going home, and another use case, food, and now I say: I am hungry. The same process starts, right? So what is the meta-workflow running in there? There is a trigger, pull versus push. Then there is enrichment: my profile, my history. Then there is exploration of what the possibilities are. Then there is optimization, based on where you are and what you like, out of all the possibilities. Let’s say there are three cab options. It will automatically pick the right cab. Then it will execute, digitally and physically. And then it learns. How was the movie? How was the ride? This is the meta-workflow. Is it universal? Will it work for everything? Yes. If you say I have fever, or if your Fitbit shows a blip, that will be a trigger, and it will say you need to see a cardiologist right now.

The meta-workflow behind every super-app request.

And the whole question right now is: to agent or not to agent, right? That’s the question Shakespeare would ask. Tell me, when will you use an agent and when will you not? What is the real skill of an agent? It is when you don’t know your workflow, and you want to discover a workflow. If you know your workflow, or if you want a deterministic answer, you don’t want an agent. And 90% of the industry wants deterministic answers, right? Imagine Amazon says deliver this packet to that location, and the LLM comes up with something creative. Or you ordered a big sofa, and it sends a red sofa. Big sofa ki jagah red sofa, right? You don’t want non-determinism. So 90% of industrial use cases are deterministic and well defined. When these two criteria are satisfied, you don’t want an agent. That’s my current understanding. Okay.

YouTube and BookMyShow never talk#

So what is the real power? When did I start thinking about it? This was eight years ago. I was watching YouTube, following this artist. And I said, wow, I like this guy. YouTube knows from my profile that I like him. Now BookMyShow knows that this artist is performing in my city. Both the apps are on my phone. But they are not talking to each other. That is the real problem we have to solve. So the third wave of digital transformation is not that YouTube is intelligent, or BookMyShow is intelligent. It is about how they are integrated. That is the next round of innovation. If I was doing both via my super-app, I would never have missed that show.

Another example of this integration mindset. Imagine you tell your super-app: I am hungry, get my favourite pizza. It will do that, right? And if you are in Bangalore and you say “my favourite pizza”, and then you go to another city and say the same thing, contextualization will happen: the same pizza, delivered from nearby, in the new city. All that intelligence can be there. But what is more important than delivering your pizza is to consult your health parameters and say: do you deserve a pizza? No, no, your cholesterol is high. This ability to do cross-domain integration is what the super-app is. People confuse it with a screen with a bunch of apps.

Another example. Think about a situation where two people are talking. What will happen after this conversation? Let’s watch a movie. Oh, I don’t like that movie. Oh, that theatre is too far. Let me check my calendar. I find a different show. All that happens now. Imagine instead the two super-apps talking to each other. They know your preferences, your location, your calendar, your behaviour. They find the perfect movie both of you would like, at the right time, the right location. And by the time you have your tickets, both your cabs are booked, because it’s all connected. You see the power of integration, between people and across applications? That is the future of the super-app.

You understand why we are talking about the super-app as an operating system. And whoever builds this first is going to win the race, right? Amazon can stay with retail forever. Swiggy can stay with food forever. But whoever builds the whole thing will win.

The AI marketplace#

So now let me talk about the AI marketplace. When we say AI today, everybody thinks about ChatGPT and all that. But we are all data scientists, so we understand better.

Think about all the sectors in the country. Each sector has many beneficiaries. Each beneficiary uses many types of applications. Each application needs a variety of AI services. There are hundreds of AI services, and who is going to build them all? ISRO cannot build all of that. Sarvam cannot build all of that.

So we need a marketplace idea. The Buddha approach is not to say: I am going to hire 10,000 data scientists and build all these apps. That’s like saying, I want to solve transportation, so I will buy cars and run them around. What would a meta thinker do? To solve transportation, a meta thinker will build an Ola. He will not buy cars. What is the equivalent of that in AI? I want to connect all the producers of AI with all the consumers of AI. Pretty much like Ola, Uber, Swiggy, YouTube, Amazon. It’s all about producers and consumers, right?

Let me show you a video. This is from Reliance Foundation. We built the Machli app for the fishermen of India. When you click on English as the language, it asks for your phone number, then the menu. Click on Telugu or any other language, and it doesn’t say anything.

So this is my perfect example of a consumer of AI. He knows everything about fish, right? He needs Indian coastal languages. He wants text-to-speech. Now imagine, somewhere in the country, somebody goes to a website and says: I just finished my PhD at IIIT Hyderabad, I have built a great Telugu text-to-speech model. And he uploads it. That’s all he does. That guy doesn’t know anything about the fisherman. The fisherman doesn’t know anything about him. That is the fundamental nature of a marketplace, right? He uploads the model, and suddenly this app speaks Telugu.

That is the power we want to create. On one side, you have consumers of AI. They don’t want to do AI, they don’t want a data science team to build a text-to-speech model for fisheries. On the other side, there are producers of AI. The API was getting called, but there was no model, until somebody uploaded one. This is one of the most important pieces of AI infrastructure India and other countries need to build. Today we have Hugging Face, valued at billions of dollars. This is an easier version of that.

The domain operating system#

The last thing I want to talk about is the domain operating system, right? Think about where the world is going with products. Products are becoming more and more complex.

The first generation of products was programmed. The second generation, your Google Search and Facebooks of the world, learned from planet-scale feedback. What did you click on? What did you search? What did you watch on YouTube? Now, for the next 30 years, we are heading into a different era of products. A smart city is a product of the future. The whole agricultural ecosystem of a country is a product. The whole healthcare ecosystem, or e-commerce ecosystem.

So you guys are actually sitting on a complex system, right? It is not simply a product. These are very complex designs right now. And the problem is this. If I am a retail company or a bank, somebody tells me: you are a retail business, go buy a search engine. So I go to one vendor and buy a search engine. Then I am told I need a supply chain system. I go to another vendor and buy a supply chain system. Imagine I bought 20 components of a handset, got all the components together, and now let me run my retail business. Is this how you can run it? But this is what most of the old companies have done. They have bought many, many vendor products that don’t talk to each other. User ID in one, customer ID in another, the name fields don’t match. Imagine putting a Ferrari car on a dirt road, right? How long will it last?

That’s what we are doing. Skipping the foundations and trying to build agents on top. So what we need is to move away from what I call digital clutter, to a proper platform. That is what a proper platform looks like.

Systems and workflows#

So, so how do we think about a complex system? I will simplify it into systems and workflows. Take airlines. A very sophisticated, very mature industry. What are the systems in the airline industry? You have a system to book a flight, a system to check in, a system for baggage handling, food, the aircraft. So you have systems for everything. And then, what are the workflows that run across these systems?

Every domain has the same kinds of systems. You have systems of record, right? Your raw data, your aggregated data, all part of the systems of record. You have systems of intelligence. You are not just training one model here and one model there. You need hundreds of models to run a complex system, right? So the complexity in ChatGPT is different. One large language model versus the complexity of hundreds of models for different types of decisions. Then you have systems of interaction: the website, the app, the DigiYatra terminal. And what is missing? The system of integration.

Whenever you show a search result, if you don’t know what is in inventory and you are showing it as a top result, you cannot build these two independently. Integration has to come in at some point, asking: what am I showing? Can I deliver that or not?

So how do you think about a system of integration? The human body has all of these. We are a complex system. Our system of intelligence is the brain. Our system of integration is the nervous system and the blood. Without them, we would be a collection of organs not talking to each other. That is what digital transformation has become. That is the real problem.

Record, intelligence and interaction already exist. What is missing is the system of integration, the nervous system that makes search ask inventory ‘can I deliver that?’

A retail example#

So let me return to a retail example, quickly. The idea is that you are collecting the raw data: search, customer care, returns, ratings, reviews, recommendations. And you are making location 360, product 360, brand 360, customer 360. Let’s say in the fashion world, you are saying this customer likes shirts, within shirts he likes these brands, within brands he likes these styles. We are building deep customer profiles, embedding-based. And now using this, when customer A, who has this profile, and customer B, who has that profile, both search for “men’s shirt”, different things should come. One guy searches “sofa” and another guy searches “sofa”, and the results are different.

Now let me talk about integration at JioMart. I can learn about the customer based on their grocery, their digital and electronics purchases, what fashion they want. I had three different parallel data-science streams, technology platforms, everything running independently. Then I said: look, if we join all the three, we can build a joint profile. And we also have a media business: songs, movies and TV shows. Can I not build a joint media 360 right now? Can I combine all that to create one customer persona? Okay. This idea of integration beyond one vertical can go on and on. If you listen to bhajans in the morning, what fashion product can I recommend? Can I recommend a kurta-pyjama to that person? That is the new insight that will come.

Five workflows in every domain#

Now let me show you the workflows, which are the same in different domains. For example, I have the execution workflow, right? How do you deliver a product? Then something suddenly starts falling. What do you do? Why is it happening? What can you do about it? That’s root cause. Then the expansion workflow. Where should we put the next store? Where should I put the next tower? Which flight should I start next? Then efficiency. How do I measure efficiency in Ola, Uber? What does it mean in a movie theatre business? And explanation. How do you explain everything to the end user? These are all very common things.

So I showed you the five common systems that every domain has, and the five or six common workflows. This is the new language you will organize your thoughts around. And if I look at these workflows, think of customer acquisition, expansion of infrastructure, product features. How does WhatsApp know what is the next feature to add? How does Netflix know what new movie to add? How do you know which next sofa to add to your inventory? Pricing, routing, all of these.

These are all examples in very simple language. So the goal is not to become an AI person, but to become a workflow person. Which you cannot become if you don’t understand the domain, right? Workflow thinking comes from the domain. Systems thinking comes from AI and IT. Unless these two teams work together, we will not get a complete operating system.

The power of integration#

So let me show you some examples of the power of integration. Let’s say we have a lot of crime in a country. We have cameras everywhere. Crimes are still happening. Police come, take out a memory stick from the camera, look at the pictures and then try to figure out what happened. Now imagine the video events are coming in, and we can analyse them in real time. We can detect actions automatically, raise alerts, detect faces, notify people, and all of that.

Why is this not happening? Do we not know how to get video out? Do we not know how to build a video detection system, a face detection system? What is missing? We have built the points. We have built cameras. We have built face detection. We have built action detection. What have we not done? Integrating them. The camera company only does cameras, the AI company only does AI, the notification company only does notifications. Somebody has to put it together. We need that kind of a mindset change.

Another example. Imagine a farmer is calling. What is the Buddha problem in agriculture? Daily advisory to every farmer. As a data scientist, I think about what AI I can do. But as a process, first I need to understand his speech. I need to build a knowledge graph of agriculture, like a product catalogue in your world. I need to build a farmer profile, like you have a customer profile. I need to integrate all the real-time sensors. And then I need to give him the information to act. So you see, this is one workflow, running across a system of interaction, which is a phone call, systems of record, two types of data, the knowledge graph and the sensors, a system of intelligence, and agricultural experts. Everything is systems thinking. So your mindset should not be that AI is the starting point. The workflow is. AI is part of that. There is nothing special about it. The hotel is the goal, not a bulb in the hotel. If you don’t know the hotel, you will not put the right bulbs in the right places.

Another side of intelligence that is happening now. Think about the doctors missing in this country, right? We don’t have enough doctors. So the hardest problem a doctor solves: a mother of a five-year-old in a small town in Odisha, and the child has a vomiting episode. What is she going to do? There is no doctor near that person. How do we think about it?

So imagine we build an AI diagnosis system. It starts to have a conversation with her, then we confirm the final diagnosis with a real doctor. Then it says what investigation you need, sends it to the path lab, all of that is integrated. The AI looks at the report, again a doctor confirms. And AI drafts the prescription, again a doctor confirms. So this is the future of how AI and humans will work together. Humans will curate what AI does. This is the shift that is happening with all of us, and I call it the curation mindset. Doctors will not be replaced, they will move up. Teachers will get this too. They are going to become curators of what AI is doing, and at the same time increase productivity. But the first problem that will happen: we will become dumber and dumber. That is what we should worry about. How do we preserve our human creativity and intelligence while AI becomes intelligent? That is the real challenge.

Work backward from the operating system#

Now, these are some examples. Now think about the retail operating system as a circle, right? One team is working on how orders can come from anywhere. Somebody walks into the store, somebody looks at an ad, somebody searches. Orders and events come in. Do we have a standard event format? Then we should draw another workflow called demand forecasting. Then we should draw a workflow called: how do I deliver? I should deliver the product from the warehouse where the forecast demand is low and inventory is high. The optimization will mature, everything will happen.

Then we have another workflow that does what we were talking about earlier, the profile. There are a hundred workflows, but I am just showing the top five.

Work backward from the operating system and say: what is missing? Is everything working or not? Then say: let me improve. So how will the retail operating system look? Think about all the events you get, whether online or offline. Think of a retail digital twin: customer, supplier, store 360. All the content, images generated, tagged, the whole knowledge base: this product has this part, this supplier. All the AI models. And now you are running all your five or six workflows on it.

And now start thinking about the AWS moment in retail. Once I have solved 20 problems that any retailer has, can I start that as a separate platform for retail? Can I now sell it as a product to all the other retail companies? Why should everybody build their own? You could be doing an AWS kind of business, and not remain a retail company.

The reason I am thinking like this is because I have looked at five or six different businesses in a conglomerate. I look at finance, I look at telecom, I look at retail. And I ask: what is the common factor across domains? Therefore, there must be a domain operating system, customized to each. So we are building one now, and in the future you can sell it to all the retail companies. Every one of them gets an OS. This is the horizontalization, not of AI, but of operating systems.

So the journeys are going to look like this. We start with networks, all the way to integrated operating systems. The workflows will have metrics, and intelligence will move to what I call emergent intelligence. This is emergent, not generative. You ordered a product, and the inventory got updated on the other side, because everything was connected.

And how we approach it matters. If we had only perfected the engine of the car for the last 200 years and never launched, we would never have had the car. Sometimes we get into building the perfect model. No. Everything should work together first. Integration first is more important, right? Integrate first, then improve. That is how evolution works. So this is a new way of working.

The clue is the amoeba. The amoeba was the first integrated life form. And after that, everything evolved. If the amoeba was not there, mitochondria would not have evolved, and we would never have lungs, right?

So you understand: integrate, then improve, is a better way than saying improve first, because then it will stop there. Thank you.

From the Q&A#

Q: If everyone is integrating, where does differentiation come from?

All of us have the same physical body. So how do we differ? The difference is the way we operate, right? Veg or non-veg, exercise or no exercise, smoking or not. Differentiation comes from our policies and how well we use our data. You can still be unique. You can fine-tune your models differently than the other guy. But the workflows are the same. Differentiation comes from the lower-level improvements, and from the data, which is owned by the company. Your customer insights are yours. That is the difference.

Let me give you an example. Your supplier doesn’t know anything about your customer, because you are sitting in the middle. Similarly, P&G doesn’t know who is buying its toothpaste. The retailer knows. So the retailers have that edge over the suppliers. The suppliers need that market insight. They can go to customers directly, but that’s not reliable. That is the moat. Your search engine pipeline, query processing, enrichment, personalization, is not unique. What is unique is the data that powers the models in that pipeline.

Q: A custom build lets you tune thousands of things, and a platform has restrictions. How do you decide between the two?

Let me talk about coding first, right? We are all celebrating AI coding. What do you think is the most important metric of code? What do you guys think? Is it how quickly you build it? No. Is it correctness? No. We are going all out on productivity, not maintainability. Five years from now, nobody in the company will understand what is going on in the code. If you want to make a small change, you will not be able to go to the right line in the code. You will prompt: please make this thing green. You will not know where in the codebase to go to make it green. That is how bad it is going. So my first advice, if you are falling for this vibe coding: make sure you have maintainability. Some people in my company should have a mind map of the entire code. Otherwise it will become a big mess.

Interoperability, not accuracy

The other goal I keep coming back to is the complex system. The goal is not that the model is great. A model can become great over time. The goal is in the complex system design. Interoperability is the goal, not accuracy. So what we do first is build an interoperable system.

In India, we understand this word, tathastu, right? You go to God and pray, and God says tathastu, so be it. We are in the tathastu era. Whatever you can imagine, you can build. Building is very fast. We are getting hyper about building something quickly, building something new tomorrow, without taking the time to think and design holistically and make sure it works with everything else. So we need to develop arrow thinking. There is box thinking and there is arrow thinking. Arrow thinking is very important. Systems thinking is very important. You can improve your search engine, but who will tell your search engine that it should consult your inventory? That is more important now than anything else. Holistically, theek hai? Integrated systems.

Box thinking vs arrow thinking.

Q: With super-apps, one entity will know everything about us. How do you see privacy turning out?

When you go to the front desk of a hotel, you have to give your ID card. But in the room, whatever you do, the person who is cleaning the room doesn’t know who you are. They know how you left the room, right? This is privacy. Privacy means your behaviour and your identity are separate. YouTube doesn’t know me as Shailesh Kumar at gmail.com. It knows me as some ABCD 1234, and it can model my behaviour all along. It doesn’t know who I am. That is the first principle.

Now let me give you two or three examples of how I learnt this. 9/11, everybody knows. I was in the US at that time, and the Department of Homeland Security said we cannot let this happen again. We need to use AI to figure out how to detect terrorist behaviour. I was part of the team. Anybody who books a flight in the US, that information should come to Homeland Security, because we want to analyse it for terrorism markers. For example, five people on the same PNR, sitting in strategic locations on a plane, in a sudden rectangle. Domain experts would start to see the patterns. So we started building that system, in the heat of the moment, and we didn’t think about privacy. We said this is important, we need to protect lives. And the American Civil Liberties Union sued Homeland Security, saying they are collecting people’s travel data. Guess who won the case? The ACLU. National security was less important. Privacy was more important. That is one example.

Second example of privacy. Gmail search auto-suggestions were poor. Why? Because auto-suggestions are built on your historical query logs, and you don’t do that many queries in Gmail. So the query logs were very poor. I was at Google. I built a system to mine your emails, with your permission, to create a list of keywords that appear frequently in your mails, and suggest from those. It went for a legal review. The privacy principle was not violated, in the sense that I didn’t know the PII of the person. I knew the email behaviour, separate from identity. But the legal review said this cannot be launched. Because even the perception that Google is bringing up your private conversations as an auto-suggestion is very bad for customers.

Third example. How many of you did DigiYatra, right? In the early days, because I came from that 9/11 world, I understood privacy differently. Now, in India, your DigiYatra is connected to your Aadhaar card. Your Aadhaar card is connected to your PAN card. Your PAN card is connected to your income tax. I used to take a hundred flights a year, every week a flight. Now imagine someone starts mining: this guy, with this PAN card, who pays this much tax, is taking urgent flights. Privacy is not a general term. It has very specific qualities. And they have to start saying: we are going to use your face image only for this, and not for anything else.

So privacy is a very important subject, and in a super-app privacy matters even more. Imagine I have your insurance stuff, your food stuff and your health stuff. I cannot reveal your health parameters to the insurance part. Your school performance cannot go to your college application. There are going to be laws like this. Doctors cannot tell insurance companies anything about a patient.

So privacy laws and regulatory frameworks are there. And there is a bigger privacy problem now. In enterprises, people are using AI coding left and right, putting code and sample errors into Codex and the rest to figure things out. That is a huge problem now. Because the race in AI is not anymore about who has the better model. The race is about who has more customers, so they have more user data to improve the models. Enterprises are revealing their IP and their product catalogs inside these tools. We should think about the privacy of the enterprise too, and ask: how do I create a firewall, so that whatever I do inside doesn’t go outside? Many companies have started looking at that as a bigger problem than token cost.

On the future

Observe it from a distance. How mankind will adopt these new, powerful technologies is itself a beautiful thing to watch. And the jobs of the future in AI will not be data scientists. AI architects and policy are going to be very important roles.
